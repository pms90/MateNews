Marsha Blackburn sues Jack Smith over seized phone records.

U.S. Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) speaks on Capitol Hill on October 07, 2025. Photo: Win McNamee/Getty Images.

Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-Tenn.) sued former special counsel Jack Smith Wednesday, alleging he unlawfully obtained her cellphone toll records during his investigation of Trump and the 2020 election

Why it matters: The lawsuit escalates Republicans' efforts to challenge Smith's investigation more than a year after his probe ended.

What they're saying: "Through his Arctic Frost witch hunt that targeted conservatives across America and even the President of the United States, Jack Smith perpetrated one of the worst abuses of government power in our nation's history," Blackburn said.

Driving the news: The suit was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee one day after Smith testified at a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing Tuesday.

Flashback: The lawsuit comes one day after Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-Mo.), a potential running mate for JD Vance in 2028, accused Smith of perjury at Tuesday's hearing but got his facts wrong and was later corrected by Smith and Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.).

Catch up quick: In October 2025, the GOP-led House Judiciary Committee released highly redacted documents showing that the FBI had investigated approximately 160 Republican figures as part of its probe into Trump's false claims that the 2020 presidential election was stolen.

By the numbers: The lawsuit claims the Arctic Frost probe subpoenaed records associated with at least 20 Republican members of Congress.

The bottom line: Blackburn's lawsuit extends the legal and political fight over the Justice Department's investigations into Trump after he left office.
