5. America is seeing an uptick in executions.

Florida executed its 14th prisoner for the year on Tuesday, adding to a post-COVID surge in capital punishment that drove more executions last year than any since 2009.

The big picture: Even as fewer courts sentence defendants to death and more than half of the states have banned or at least temporarily halted the practice, the Trump administration has overtly cheered on the death penalty.

By the numbers: Forty-seven people were executed last year, 19 of whom were in Florida, according to the DPI's database dating back to 1977.

Zoom in: Florida has led the way with executions, including two men being put to death within hours of one another back in July. It was the first time the state had done so in more than six decades.

Catch up quick: While there has not been a federal execution since 2021, President Trump signed a day-one executive order instructing his attorney general to pursue the death penalty and ensure states have a sufficient supply of drugs for lethal injections.

Yes, but: DPI's data doesn't point to a wave of executions that could rival '90s or 2000s levels. Fewer than 25 people were sentenced to death in 2025 per DPI's tracker, compared to numbers well into the 100s in the 2000s.

Friction point: That aligns with slipping public support for the death penalty, per Gallup's trend , which hit its lowest level since 1972 (52%) last year.

What we're watching: Florida is set to add to its tally again, with an execution set for September 10.
