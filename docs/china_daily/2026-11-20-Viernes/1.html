Alignment of regional legal rules highlighted.

Legal professionals from China and members of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations have called for closer alignment of regional trade rules and standards to foster an open, inclusive and rules-based integrated market, as the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0 Upgrade Protocol is set to take effect on Dec 1.

They spoke at the eighth China-ASEAN Legal Cooperation Forum, held in Chongqing on Thursday, where participants discussed ways to strengthen legal cooperation to support regional prosperity and stability.

Hosted by the China Law Society, the forum brought together about 200 senior officials, experts, lawyers and business representatives from 13 countries to exchange views on topics including the cultivation of legal talent, judicial cooperation in developing the New International Land-Sea Trade Corridor, cross-border crime, anti-corruption and risk management for enterprises expanding overseas.

China has been ASEAN's largest trading partner for 17 consecutive years, while ASEAN has been China's largest trading partner for six consecutive years.

From January through July this year, bilateral trade reached $744.41 billion, up 24.7 percent year-on-year, according to the Ministry of Commerce.

Wang Hongxiang, executive vice-president of the China Law Society, said legal communities in China and ASEAN countries should seize the opportunity to strengthen alignment in rules, regulations, management practices and standards.

"This will reduce institutional transaction costs across the region, facilitate the free flow of innovation resources and support the development of a higher-standard, integrated regional market," he said at the opening ceremony.

Wang Shumei, a second-rank justice and a standing member of the Supreme People's Court's adjudication committee, said the top court has in recent years participated in revising a series of laws to align them with high-standard international rules and further strengthen the rule of law in foreign-related affairs.

Ly Tayseng, vice-chair of the legal and justice council under Cambodia's Ministry of Justice, said economic integration without adequate legal cooperation could create uncertainty, increase transaction costs and expose businesses to unresolved disputes.

"We should therefore strengthen cooperation in commercial law, international arbitration, judicial assistance, contract enforcement and cross-border dispute resolution," he said.

He said that cooperation with China offers Cambodia important opportunities to exchange legal expertise, strengthen judicial capacity, improve commercial dispute-resolution mechanisms and create a more predictable investment environment.

The cultivation of legal professionals with cross-cultural expertise is also a pressing priority for China-ASEAN cooperation.

Southwest University of Political Science and Law, based in Chongqing, has launched a pilot Belt and Road program to train legal professionals specializing in regional and country-specific legal studies.

"We have trained nearly 4,000 high-caliber foreign-related legal professionals, with a focus on ASEAN, making the university the country's largest institution for cultivating China-ASEAN legal talent," said Tang Qingyang, Party secretary of the university.

Since 2016, the university's training program has enrolled 126 ASEAN students, who have since served as a special bond between the two sides, he said.
