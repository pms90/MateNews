New York has a socialist mayor. Los Angeles may be next.

Mayoral candidate Nithya Raman speaking at Axios/Noticias Telemundo Visionarios on Sept. 30. Photo: Ben Camacho for Axios

LOS ANGELES — Voters could make history next month by giving the two largest U.S. cities something in common: South Asian immigrant democratic socialist mayors.

Why it matters: Such a pairing would capture two forces reshaping urban America — its growing diversity and the Democratic Party's shifting ideological boundaries.

State of play: Raman became the first Asian American woman and first South Asian elected to the L.A. City Council in 2020.

The intrigue: The Berkeley poll had Raman ahead 66%-4% among likely voters under 30, while 56% rated Bass' response to the Palisades Fire poor or very poor.

What they're saying: "I'm getting a little emotional thinking about it," Raman told Axios and Noticias Telemundo on Wednesday at Visionarios when asked what it would mean if she and Mamdani led the country's two largest cities.

The other side: Bass strategist Julie Chavez Rodriguez told Axios and Noticias Telemundo that voters are talking about rent, affordable housing, public safety and whether they can afford to raise families in Los Angeles.

Zoom in: Raman's version of democratic socialism sounds less revolutionary than municipal.

Zoom out: Mamdani has made a similar ideological bet in New York, declaring at his inauguration that he was elected as a democratic socialist and would govern as one.

Yes, but: Raman isn't simply the West Coast version of Mamdani.
