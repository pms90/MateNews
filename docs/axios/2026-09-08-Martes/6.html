How 2026 candidates are targeting health care "corruption".

Campaigning on health care used to mean talking about coverage or costs . Now, in the run-up to the midterms, it's just as likely to focus on "corruption."

The big picture: Candidates are accusing primary or general election opponents of being in the pockets of drugmakers, insurers and other powerful health interests, to the detriment of voters.

Zoom in: That thinking is influencing the tone of one of the most competitive House races in the country, in Iowa's 1st Congressional District. GOP Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks and Democrat Christina Bohannan, along with their surrogates, have accused each other of caving to big health insurers and undermining patient care.

Between the lines: Polling shows medical costs are the public's top economic worry .

Flashback: GOP lawmakers in Congress and outside conservative groups portrayed the Affordable Care Act as a profit-making machine for health insurers last year as they rallied to allow the expiration of pandemic-era enhanced subsidies.

What's next: House Democrats have already vowed to reverse the Medicaid cuts in the reconciliation bill and restore the enhanced ACA subsidies. But there's an added oversight component some are talking up on the campaign trail.

What we're watching: Whether Republicans take Trump's advice to go on offense by making insurance companies the enemy in the final months before the midterms.
