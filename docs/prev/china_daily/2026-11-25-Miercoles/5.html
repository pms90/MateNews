Rules drafted for textbook management.

New framework sets stricter standards for content, pricing and distribution

China has drafted a new regulation to strengthen textbook management across the entire chain, from planning and writing to review, publication, distribution, selection and use, as part of efforts to build a high-quality textbook system with Chinese characteristics.

The draft, formulated by the Ministry of Education and released on Monday, comprises eight chapters and 67 articles and is open for public opinion until Dec 24.

An accompanying explanation said the draft is intended to turn mature and effective practices into laws and regulations and provide a legal foundation for the high-quality development of textbook work.

The draft states that textbook work must uphold the leadership of the Communist Party of China and the socialist direction of education, reflect the will of the Party and the State, and promote core socialist values.

It would establish a national textbook committee under the State Council to guide and coordinate textbook work nationwide, with provincial-level committees responsible for their respective regions. The Ministry of Education would take the lead in national textbook work, while other departments would share responsibility.

The draft sets strict requirements for textbook content, saying textbooks must be politically sound, scientifically accurate and suitable for teaching. They may not violate Party and State policies, go against public order and good morals, or contain commercial advertisements or links to commercial activities. Textbooks may not be published, printed or distributed with advertisements or bundled supplementary materials.

It also tightens qualifications for textbook writers and publishers, requires chief editors to take responsibility for all content, and calls for timely revisions when major theoretical or practical developments occur or when curricula and teaching standards change. Writers would also be required to seek feedback from teachers, students and experts.

A national review system would be applied to all textbooks, with greater emphasis on political vetting. Reviewers must meet relevant qualifications and may not review textbooks they helped write or revise. They must also recuse themselves in cases involving conflicts of interest.

National curriculum textbooks for primary and secondary schools would be approved by the Ministry of Education, while local curriculum textbooks would be approved by provincial education departments.

The draft also addresses textbook pricing and distribution. Textbook prices should follow the principle of covering costs while allowing modest profits, and primary and secondary school textbooks must arrive before the start of classes.

It requires selected foreign textbooks to comply with relevant State regulations and undergo review and oversight to ensure correct orientation, scientific accuracy and advanced applicability. At the same time, it calls for stronger international exchanges and cooperation to promote high-quality Chinese textbooks abroad.

The draft establishes an exit mechanism for textbooks. Those with problems in political direction or value orientation must be immediately suspended, while those found to be of poor quality, outdated in content or poorly evaluated would no longer be selected.

To support the system, the draft reaffirms free textbooks for students in compulsory education, calls for stronger protection of textbook intellectual property rights, and requires publishers to report textbook-related data to the Ministry of Education within 60 days of publication.

It also calls on society to create a favorable environment for the healthy development of textbook work. No organization or individual may produce, reproduce, publish or disseminate false or misleading information concerning textbooks, according to the draft.

The draft sets out penalties for violations, including orders to stop using textbooks, warnings, confiscation of illegal gains, fines and temporary bans on textbook writing.
