Wuhan revitalizes idle, messy under-bridge spaces.

Spaces beneath bridges, once regarded as the "gray corners" of cities — dirty, disorderly and often neglected — have been transformed into new venues for culture, entertainment and sports in Wuhan, Hubei province, improving the urban environment and enriching residents' lives.

The transformation may offer a model for other cities seeking to make better use of idle spaces to benefit residents while tapping new business potential in urban development, experts said.

He Tingting, 38, a resident of Optics Valley, said the space beneath an overpass along the Third Ring Road used to be a shabby mess, cluttered with makeshift structures.

"The roads were in poor condition, turning muddy whenever it rained," she said.

Today, the area has been transformed into a public park. Huge bridge piers are covered with vibrant murals, children line up for the slides and elderly residents sing beneath the bridge. The roar of cars speeding overhead, the screech of trains on nearby tracks, the singing of elderly people and children's laughter blend into a symphony.

The transformation took only 60 days. Local authorities cleared weeds and debris, transplanted more than 1,300 saplings and connected previously fragmented green spaces into one continuous stretch. Drainage pipelines prone to waterlogging during rainy seasons were renovated to address inundation risks, while new pedestrian and cycling paths were laid.

"I often bring my kid here. It is spacious and has playground facilities, so my child can easily spend half a day playing. The elderly in my family also come here regularly for walks," He said.

Local authorities are cooperating with China Resources Land in operating the site and plan to introduce leisure businesses such as camping, light dining and cafes.

Elsewhere, the under-bridge space on the Wuchang bank of the Second Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge was once filled with grass, dirt and randomly parked cars. Now called Wuyin Park, the 8,000-square-meter space features dining facilities and a children's playground, as well as newly planted flowers and colorful stores.

"The whole family can spend an entire day here," said Liu Lihua, 68, another resident. "I'm really delighted to see this change in our city, and all the neighbors praise it."

Wuhan has 878 urban bridges and tunnels, including grand bridges spanning the Yangtze River and its branches and connecting the districts of Hanyang, Hankou and Wuchang, as well as numerous overpasses.

As part of an urban upgrading program, the city has completed renovations of under-bridge spaces at 14 sites, with seven more projects planned, according to the Wuhan Commission of Urban Management and Law Enforcement.

Beneath the Hankou approach of the Erqi Yangtze River Bridge lies a 12,100-sq-m space.

Once cluttered with junk and shrouded in gloom, it has been renovated into a sports park featuring public sports grounds, sports venues and a smart parking lot.

Outdoor basketball courts and table tennis tables, along with a comprehensive service center, are open to the public free of charge. The park also features indoor courts for tennis, badminton and table tennis.

Since its trial operation in September, the tennis center has recorded more than 2,000 visits, with a booking rate exceeding 90 percent during the National Day holiday in October, according to local authorities.

"The park not only fills the gap in the area's lack of large-scale sports facilities but also drives riverside business and ecological leisure," said Zhang Tongwen, deputy director of the commission.

In another example, a soccer field, two pickleball courts, parking lots and EV charging stations were built beneath a circular overpass. Elsewhere, six elevated bridge piers were painted with historical information, while an under-bridge square was turned into a space where residents and officials can sit down to discuss community affairs and urban development.

Zhang said the design of the under-bridge spaces is based on surveys of residents to better meet their needs, adding that the commission will further standardize management of the spaces.

Other cities, including Beijing, Shenyang in Liaoning province and Leshan in Sichuan province, have also launched similar programs, transforming idle spaces beneath bridges into cinemas, fresh markets, night markets and basketball courts.

Li Dong, a professor at Tsinghua University's Institute for Urban Governance and Sustainable Development, said the Central Urban Work Conference clearly stated that China's urban development is shifting from a stage of large-scale incremental expansion to one focused on improving the quality and efficiency of existing urban space.

"Wuhan's revitalization of underbridge spaces serves as a vivid example of putting these guiding principles into practice that can be learned by other large cities that usually also have numerous such gray spaces," he said.

Li also suggested that governments cooperate with professional operation teams and introduce commercial businesses to make such public welfare programs more economically sustainable in the long run.
