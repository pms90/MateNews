1. Trump escalates war on federal data.

The nerdy, traditionally apolitical world of federal data is increasingly getting swept up in the Trump administration's push to control every level of government.

Why it matters: Reliable, unbiased data informs nearly everything the federal government does — from distributing benefits and enforcing workplace discrimination laws to responding to public health threats.

Driving the news: The administration's moves in recent weeks span an array of agencies and types of data.

🩺 Measles. Reuters reported that Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. asked the CDC director to remove a reference to two deaths in Pennsylvania from its online tally of measles cases.

🏡 Census Bureau. Last month, the Commerce Department did away with language barring political interference that had been part of the department's scientific integrity policy , NPR reported . The department oversees the Census Bureau and several other agencies.

⚠️ Anti-discrimination. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission is planning to stop collecting data that's helped the agency investigate claims of workplace discrimination, The Guardian reported .

What they're saying: "The level of politicization in this administration is beyond anything I've seen," says Denice Ross, former U.S. chief data scientist and currently director of the Data Policy Institute at the Federation of American Scientists. "I've been doing this work for 25 years."

Zoom out: Fears about the handling of data that doesn't comport with Trump's worldview have been around for years .

The other side: "The Trump administration is committed to providing the American people with timely, accurate, and relevant data that's helpful to inform meaningful research, discourse, and decision-making," White House spokesman Kush Desai said.

The bottom line: "We really take data for granted," Ross said. "It's invisible infrastructure, like a bridge or cell service. We don't notice until it's gone."
