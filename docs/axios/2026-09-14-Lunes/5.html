5. California attempts to crack down on health spending.

California has decided to play hardball with health care costs, adding steep penalties to enforce state-directed caps on per capita spending increases.

Why it matters: The move could prompt other states to follow, but also serve as a warning of the trade-offs that come with forcing providers to make do with less money.

Driving the news: California recently adopted steep penalties for hospitals, physician groups and insurers that exceed previously set caps on health spending.

The big picture: Caps are one of the ways that states are responding to ever-increasing health care costs — and the affordability issues compounded by those increases.

Yes, but: California's approach could wind up being more heavy-handed than those other states have taken.

The other side: Hospitals are warning that the growth caps will come with steep consequences, particularly regarding access to care.

Between the lines: Hospitals' financial situations vary significantly, and wealthier systems are much better situated to weather financial pressure than those with already-stretched margins.

The state hospital association argues that most of hospitals' costs are outside of their control, meaning there are limited areas where they can find efficiencies.

What we're watching: Whether other states follow suit — or whether the industry pushback scares them off.

The bottom line: "It's crystal clear we have an affordability crisis in this country," Corlette said. "You have to look at where the money is in the system, and the number one culprit here is hospital prices, and it's been that way for quite some time."
