1. Axios interview: Sam Altman's sobering siren.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is interviewed by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Tuesday. Photo: Jonathan Drake/Reuters

CHAPEL HILL, N.C. — OpenAI CEO Sam Altman gave Axios an eye-opening preview of the power and the risks of coming AI models, including his own Astra , which is expected to be released any day now.

Why it matters: This is a big week. Some of the world's most powerful AI voices are warning of even more ominous AI behavior in the weeks or months ahead.

Altman — facing scrutiny over a shocking breach by a swarm of OpenAI agents that went rogue during testing and hacked the AI company Hugging Face — sees a new normal in which companies like his will have to slow releases so their controls can catch up with their models' capability.

Zoom out: On top of Altman's comments, Dean Ball — a former Trump administration official who's now OpenAI's head of strategic futures, and one of the most closely followed AI voices on X — posted a provocative memo , "On the Loose: The Coming of Userless Agents," foreseeing "sovereign agents."

Altman wants OpenAI to be seen as "the most responsible company ... good stewards of technology," he told Alex Heath .

"I don't want to go too doomer here," Altman told Axios just before addressing the summit.

Using a new phrase you'll hear again, Altman told Lutnick onstage: "We are the pragmatic centrists. We want to be accurate in how we talk about what we see in the future. We don't want to fall into the trap of blind optimism. We don't want to fall into doomerism."

What we're watching: Altman told us he "wouldn't say, based on what happened with Hugging Face, that it's like one incident that people can project all sorts of things onto."
