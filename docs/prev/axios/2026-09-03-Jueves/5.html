5. House Democrats erupt into infighting over leadership power play.

Reps. Katherine Clark, Hakeem Jeffries and Pete Aguilar speak at a press conference at the U.S. Capitol on March 27. Photo: Samuel Corum/Getty Images

House Democratic leaders are trying to flex some muscle this week after two members defected on a key procedural vote. It's stoking fury among members across the political spectrum.

Why it matters: The blowup offers a preview of the chaos that party leaders could face in January if they take back the House majority.

Driving the news: Reps. Jared Golden (D-Maine) and Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-Wash.) touched off a flurry of anger from fellow Democrats on Tuesday by helping Republicans pass a procedural measure known as a "rule."

Several proposed responses have been floated behind the scenes, lawmakers and aides told Axios.

Yes, but: Several senior Congressional Progressive Caucus members told Axios they would strongly oppose such a rules change.

Between the lines: Progressives are "furious" about the proposal, said the senior House Democrat who spoke to Axios anonymously, in part because many moderates in swing districts are exempt from paying DCCC dues.

What they're saying: House Democratic Caucus chair Pete Aguilar (D-Calif.) told Axios "it's up to the caucus ... to decide if they want to vote on a rule and what that rule looks like."

Some centrists, meanwhile, said the idea of removing Golden or Gluesenkamp Perez from committees would be a slap in the face to their wing of the party.

"If they're going to apply it only to us, hell yeah that would be a big problem for everybody," battleground district Rep. Vicente Gonzalez (D-Texas) told Axios.

The bottom line: Many lawmakers speculated that this episode was leadership's way of trying to signal a new, more authoritative style in anticipation of taking back the House majority.

Editor's note: This story has been updated to clarify a comment from a centrist Democrat about a rule vote in 2022.
