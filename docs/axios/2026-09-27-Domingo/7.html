Treasury yields are soaring, with the 30-year Treasury bond climbing to its highest level since 2004 Thursday.

The big picture: For the bond market, these moves have been big. Bigly even.

Flashback: Markets heads remember that it was ructions in the bond market — "They were getting yippy ," the president famously said at the time — that prompted Trump to walk back some of the most extreme tariff policies.

Yes, but: This time, the dynamics driving the bond market are more complicated, global and difficult to manage.

Case in point: The big driver of Wednesday's surge in yields — which means, remember, that bond prices were falling — was one of the earliest economic reports on the U.S. economy in September.

Zoom out: Inflation is anathema for bond market investors as it erodes the value of the interest payments bondholders collect, making them less attractive assets.

Between the lines: Lurking in the background of the inflation-driven market move is the specter of a U.S. diesel export ban, which the Trump administration is reportedly considering .

What they're saying: "The proposed U.S. diesel export ban will not play out as the U.S. administration expects," wrote Susan Bell, an oil analyst with the consulting firm Rystad Energy. "While it may temporarily lower domestic diesel prices, it will cause the prices for all other refined products to soar as U.S. refineries cut run rates to balance their diesel production with the domestic demand."

The bottom line: The inflationary pressures appear to be building in the economy, which will be a headache both for politicians in the final stretch of the midterm elections and for policymakers like Federal Reserve chairman Kevin Warsh.
