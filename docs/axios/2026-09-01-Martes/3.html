3. Johnson's unavoidable, uncomfortable September problem.

House Speaker Mike Johnson walks to the House Chamber on Monday. Photo: Finn Gomez / Getty Images

With the House back in session after a five-week break, Speaker Mike Johnson can no longer avoid an issue he's skirted all summer: allegations of sexual misconduct in the GOP.

Why it matters: The House will vote Tuesday on censuring Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-N.C.), putting Johnson and his Republican colleagues in an uncomfortable — and unavoidable — position nine weeks from Election Day.

Between the lines: GOP leadership is not whipping the Edwards vote, leaving Republicans to decide for themselves whether to impose one of the chamber's most serious punishments on their colleague.

Driving the news: Edwards mounted a last-ditch campaign to save himself from censure, circulating a 22-page letter to lawmakers arguing that the committee made an "extraordinary leap" in determining that his conduct constituted sexual harassment.

Zoom out: It will also be the first time the House has been in session since allegations of domestic violence against Rep. Max Miller (R-Ohio) exploded into public view. Miller has denied the allegations.

Rep. Cory Mills (R-Fla.) lost his primary this month but remains under an active Ethics Committee investigation over allegations that center on domestic abuse , stolen valor and financial misconduct , all of which he denies.

The other side: Democrats also will confront fresh ethical scrutiny within their ranks.

What's next: The Democratic and Republican women's caucuses are expected to unveil their legislative package in the next couple of weeks to tackle sexual misconduct on Capitol Hill.
