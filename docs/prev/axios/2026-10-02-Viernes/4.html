4. Why Washington and Tehran keep talking past each other.

Photo Illustration: Brendan Lynch/Axios. Photos: Getty Images

The Trump administration and the Iranian regime both say they prefer a diplomatic solution to the war , but a deal looks more elusive than ever.

Why it matters: In the last 18 months, two failed rounds of negotiations have led to two wars. The June MOU collapsed within days and led to renewed escalation. If the current effort to reach a diplomatic breakthrough ends in a deadlock, another outbreak of fighting is likely within weeks.

One reason for the failures is that the U.S. and Iran see the timeframe for negotiations very differently. Trump is all about getting a quick deal — he gets impatient and frustrated when things move slowly. The Iranian negotiation style is the complete opposite. They are all about playing for time.

Trump loves a splashy show of political theater, with him cast as lead actor. He envisions a legacy-making accord that redefines the relationship between the U.S. and Iran and reshapes the Middle East.

The fundamental impediment in the negotiations is mistrust.

The negotiations are a fraught domestic political issue for both sides.

What to watch: White House envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner and Vice President Vance still think diplomacy is worth pursuing. But Trump seems much more skeptical.

What they're saying: "Iran is adopting a strategy toward the United States defined by escalation and continuous offense," Mohammad Ayatollahi Tabaar, associate professor of international affairs at Texas A&M University's Bush School of Government and Public Service, writes in a new Foreign Affairs article .
