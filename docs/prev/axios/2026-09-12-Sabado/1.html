Anthropic report: 5 ways Claude was exploited for war, spying and repression.

The AI safety debate exploded this week over warnings that the technology could one day destroy humanity.

Why it matters: AI is tearing down barriers that have long constrained the world's most dangerous actors. A handful of people can now mount operations that once required legions of spies, engineers or hackers.

Driving the news: Anthropic — the safety-focused lab behind Claude — says it spent the past eight months tracking and disrupting attempts to misuse its models. Among the most alarming cases it uncovered:

1. An Iran-linked operation used Claude to help target U.S. naval forces.

2. Claude served as an engineer for a Yemeni team building missiles.

3. A China-linked operation used Claude to hunt Uyghurs .

4. One man used Claude to help build surveillance for 25 million phones.

5. Claude refused dangerous virus research — so the request went to another AI.

Between the lines: Frontier AI labs are becoming unlikely intelligence agencies in their own right.

The big picture : This week has lit a fire under Washington, with lawmakers suddenly treating AI safety as an urgent political issue.
