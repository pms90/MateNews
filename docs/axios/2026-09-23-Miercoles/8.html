President Trump feels and acts invincible : He acts on whims, however he pleases, precedent and restraints be damned.

Why it matters: Time, power and support wane for all lame-duck presidents. But it's new for Trump and will only grow more true and more torturous, assuming Democrats win Capitol power in the midterms, 43 days from now.

This is what the beginning of the end looks like, which is what scares Trump advisers and confidants most. Trump often retreats to his worst impulses when he feels most powerless.

How Trump's brain works: The skeleton key for unlocking Trump's mind is understanding that he thinks constantly about exercising power, and believes power is a zero-sum game — a winner and a loser.

Trump announced at 7:30 a.m. on Sunday that he wants his planned golden arch to have G.I. Joe-style capabilities "to house, store, and have the rapid ability to use large numbers of drones, plus Snipers, on both the roof and plaza areas, and additionally have and hold large quantities of sniper ammunition in storage." There's a method to it all: If the Arc de Trump is for national security, the courts and Congress will have a tougher time reining in the executive.

Expect more of this. Trump will act out to exert what control he can because he's lost the political argument — if not the military one — as his Iran war constantly puts the lie to his claims, raises fuel costs and, in tandem with his unpopular tariffs, saddles voters with inflation.

Behind the scenes: Trump advisers and confidants describe him as generally in a good mood. But some privately confess he can get too cranky too quickly sometimes and that he's showing his 80 years on Earth.

Between the lines: House Dems can practically taste the majority, and they're showing signs of just how much they'll investigate Trump's administration and family .

Podcaster Katie Miller , a Trump 1.0 vet and wife of White House deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller, wrote Saturday on X in response to a post about looming pardons: "After the first Trump administration, my family spent money we did not have on lawyers because of multiple partisan Democrat led investigations into President Trump."

As the first president who was impeached twice, Trump began war-gaming last year for an impeachment fight in 2027-28. As long as the GOP maintains Senate control or as long as there isn't the threat of removal by a two-thirds Senate vote after an impeachment, Trump is signaling he's ready for battle.

The bottom line: Yes, Trump commands loyalty or at least blind silence from most elected Republicans. They fear him. But with each passing day, Democrats, the courts, critics — even some congressional Republicans — feel more capable of restraining Trump and are starting to think about what comes after him.
