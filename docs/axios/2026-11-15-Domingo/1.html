How Nvidia's scandal knocked thousands off your 401(k).

Nvidia's near-30% stock plunge in the last month is a drag on the retirement savings of millions of Americans, many who may not realize just how much their investments are tied to the scandal-engulfed chip giant.

Why it matters: Nvidia's enormous weight in popular index funds means even those who have never purchased a single share of the company could feel the pain of its corporate upheaval in their 401(k)s.

By the numbers: Index funds held nearly half of the assets in large private-sector 401(k) plans in 2023, according to the Investment Company Institute.

Zoom in : For an investor with $100,000 in an S&P 500 fund, a 30% drop in Nvidia shares would knock about $2,400 off the value of that investment given an 8% S&P holding, assuming other stocks in the fund stayed flat.

Yes, but: Remember, a drop in your investment's value doesn't mean you've actually lost that cash — unless you sell your holdings.

Zoom out: The potential fallout extends beyond Nvidia itself.

Reality check: While Nvidia has come to define the AI trade, its competitors have also emerged as potential beneficiaries of the stock's decline.

Reality check: For those who are direct shareholders, the widely held financial advice would always tell you not to lock in losses as a stock drops.

The bottom line: Americans don't need to own Nvidia shares directly to have their retirement savings tied to the company's successes, or failures.
