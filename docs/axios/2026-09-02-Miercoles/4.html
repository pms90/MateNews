4. Rogue House Democrats face growing push for "consequences".

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries speaks to reporters at the U.S. Capitol on Aug. 31. Photo: Finn Gomez/Getty Images

Two centrist House Democrats who voted to help Republicans get a procedural motion across the finish line are now facing down possible punishment from party leaders who say they were blindsided by the move.

Why it matters: House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) is taking an uncharacteristically aggressive public approach to the defections, to the thrill of some of his members.

Catch up quick: Reps. Jared Golden (D-Maine) and Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-Wash.) voted with Republicans to pass what is called a "rule" — a procedural measure that sets the terms for House debate on a package of bills.

What they're saying: Jeffries, in a joint statement with Minority Whip Katherine Clark (D-Mass.) and Democratic Caucus chair Pete Aguilar (D-Calif.) released Tuesday night, signaled plans to take action.

The other side: Golden defended his vote by noting that the rule also contained a bill he co-authored to shield lobstermen from federal regulations protecting whales.

What we're hearing: A "general conversation about consequences" sprang up among members almost immediately after the vote on Tuesday, a second House Democrat, who spoke on the condition of anonymity, told Axios.

Between the lines: Golden and Gluesenkamp Perez are facing intense anger from colleagues, who feel they snatched a potential win from the party with just a few months until the November midterm elections.

Reality check: Even members who are furious with Golden and Gluesenkamp Perez acknowledge any action would likely be symbolic and, could, in the case of the latter, backfire politically.

Between the lines: While Jeffries and Clark were together on the joint statement, sources said there was significant tension between their offices in the immediate aftermath of Tuesday's vote.

The bottom line: "I think if you throw all of your colleagues under the bus by voting with the other party on a procedural rule vote, you should lose your committee assignments and get reassigned to the arts and crafts committee," said Huffman.
