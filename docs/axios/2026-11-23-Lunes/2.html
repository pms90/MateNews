How Buttigieg and Klobuchar went from enemies to friends.

Photo illustration: Brendan Lynch / Axios. Photos: Stephen Maturen and Mario Tama / Getty Images

To the surprise of many Democrats and even some of their former aides, Pete Buttigieg and Amy Klobuchar now consider themselves friends after their bitter clashes during their campaigns for president in 2020.

Why it matters: It's also a reflection of how Buttigieg — who's weighing another run for president — has become a well-regarded Democratic stalwart after many in the party viewed him as a presumptuous lightweight when he ran six years ago as a little-known 37-year-old former mayor of South Bend, Ind.

Flashback: A casual observer might see that as a bit of revisionist history.

Buttigieg repeatedly mocked Klobuchar for not remembering the name of the president of Mexico in an interview.

Asked today about those mutual attacks, Klobuchar says simply: "We both wanted to win."

Zoom in: Klobuchar said their relationship has become increasingly warm through the years.

What they're saying: A former Klobuchar aide told Axios that it's a "very Midwestern friendship story."

What's next: Klobuchar, who has said she's not running for president, didn't want to weigh in on 2028 because she knows many people who are likely to run.
