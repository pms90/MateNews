USPS whistleblower warns new ballot system could fail.

Ballots to be sorted at a polling location inside City Hall during a primary election in San Francisco, California, US, on Tuesday, June 2, 2026. Photographer: David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images

A U.S. Postal Service whistleblower is alleging the agency defied court orders while rushing to build a new ballot system that could reject thousands of mail ballots .

The big picture: The whistleblower says the Postal Service built the system in weeks, not the nine months to a year normally needed for a project of this complexity.

The latest: Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) sent a letter Monday to Postmaster General David Steiner with the whistleblower report, demanding that the agency explain its processes and whether it followed court orders to cease work on the new system.

Between the lines: USPS has historically issued guidance on election mail, such as envelope recommendations, but Blumenthal wrote that the agency has never imposed requirements that could lead it to refuse to deliver ballots.

"We urge you to abandon this ill-conceived, unconscionable plan and ensure that all Americans can exercise their constitutional right to vote, including by mail, without interference by USPS," he wrote.

What they're saying: "Last night @OversightDems received an alarming whistleblower report from a USPS federal official that President Trump's attack on vote-by-mail is much more serious than previously known," House Oversight Committee ranking member Robert Garcia (D-Calif.) said in a statement.

"The process of building software to carry out this new verification check is neither complex nor unique for USPS since the Postal Service regularly uses bulk mailing and intelligent mail barcodes for a wide variety of large customers," White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said.

For the record: The Postal Service has not yet returned a request for comment. Blumenthal's office said it has similarly not yet heard back from USPS.

The legal battle over mail-in ballots is still underway, with 23 states and Washington, D.C. suing the administration in an effort to block Trump's executive order.

The bottom line: The claim puts a new spotlight on whether USPS can implement a major new ballot-screening system this close to an election, and whether it was building that system while courts told the agency to stop.

Editor's note: This story has been updated with a comment from Rep. Robert Garcia and the White House.
