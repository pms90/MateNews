1. "We're stuck:" GOP accepts Trump's all-about-me midterms.

Photo illustration: Sarah Grillo/Axios. Photo: Saul Loeb/AFP via Getty Images

President Trump is hitting the campaign trail and doing the last thing many Republicans want: making the 59 days to the Nov. 3 midterms a referendum on him.

Why it matters : Most Republicans are too scared to bluntly tell Trump how unpopular he is. And Trump wants an unprecedented convention next week, ads and events to focus on him.

The big picture : Their timidity aside, Republicans need Trump's low-propensity voters to turn out. So they're going along with a Trump-focused strategy that many believe could cost them credibility — along with seats in the House and Senate.

Driving the news: At Trump's urging, the GOP on Wednesday and Thursday is hosting an unprecedented midterm convention in Dallas, headlined by the president.

Zoom in : Trump plans to speak both nights at the convention. He's also the star of his political operation's first two TV ads:

Inside the room: Trump essentially demanded the ads focus on him because midterms are referendums on a president anyway, and he wanted to tout his accomplishments, The Atlantic first reported.

The intrigue: The ads look like a de facto compromise between Trump and his political staff.

What he's saying : On Friday, Trump acknowledged to reporters that "history has said that the sitting president" sees midterm losses, but he didn't complete the thought and then talked about how "nobody's ever had a first one and a half years like we have."

Yes, but: It's unclear how Trump could spend that much money in two months.

Between the lines: Trump's message in his tax cut ads is good, Republican strategists say, but their research increasingly shows voters blame him for the war in Iran that's raising gas prices.

The backstory: Trump explained this week on the "Lt. Dan" podcast with Texas Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick that he decided to hold the event in Dallas because "we have this big convention for the presidential election, but the midterms — people talk about it. It's very important, but there's no convention."

Friction point : Trump's plummeting poll numbers aside, one problem is the convention's timing: It's competing with the NFL's season-opening games, which could draw far more attention.

What's next: At a Rose Garden dinner Wednesday with dozens of House Republicans, Trump promised to campaign across the country in 35 races "to see if we can get everyone elected."
