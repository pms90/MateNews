Far-right win in state election tests Germany's political firewall.

Ulrich Siegmund speaks to reporters after exit polls showed the AfD leading Saxony-Anhalt's state election. Photo: Jan Woitas/dpa via Getty Images

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) won a record 44% in Saxony-Anhalt on Sunday, leaving a small party with seats that could determine whether it can lead the nation's first far-right state government since World War II.

Why it matters: Germany rebuilt its democracy after Nazi rule with unusually strong safeguards against extremism. AfD's breakthrough shows those barriers aren't stopping Europe's far right from becoming dominant electoral forces.

The latest: AfD more than doubled its 20.8% share in 2021, hitting its highest vote share ever in a state election and finishing ahead of the center-right CDU at 17%.

Yes, but: The arithmetic may not be enough.

Catch up quick: AfD has won the most votes once before , in 2024, but it has never governed.

Flashback: Trump allies' support for the far-right party became a transatlantic flashpoint during Germany's 2025 federal election.

Between the lines: Saxony-Anhalt's intelligence service has classified the state AfD as a right-wing extremist organization.

The big picture: Europe's far right is moving in sharply different directions.

Context: Germany's postwar system was built in the shadow of the Nazis' destruction of the democratic Weimar Republic.
