1. Inside the backlash over the Jeffries-Kushner meeting.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries speaks at a press conference at the U.S. Capitol on July 23. Photo: Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries ' long-standing tensions with his party's left flank resurfaced this week after news broke of his meeting with President Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner.

Why it matters: Jeffries will have to make nice with the left next year if Democrats win back the House with only a narrow majority. Blowups like this are not helping.

What they're saying: The meeting was "definitely a signal about the kind of Speaker Jeffries would be," said Joseph Geevarghese, executive director of the progressive group OurRevolution.

Jeffries, a onetime co-sponsor of Medicare for All who dropped off the bill when he joined party leadership, said in a later radio interview that health care must "be a right available to every single American."

What we're hearing: House Democrats and Democratic congressional aides, speaking on the condition of anonymity to offer candid thoughts about their party's leader, questioned why Jeffries met with someone who has no official role in the administration.

Between the lines: "They have a relationship," a senior House Democrat told Axios of Jeffries and Kushner, noting that they "worked on criminal justice" together during Trump's first term .

The other side: "The media is trying to make this a big deal, but members are trying to enjoy the last week of August recess. Lots of CODELS and some are working," said the senior House Democrat.

The bottom line: "It's less who you meet with than what you do with the meeting," said Adam Green, the co-founder of the Progressive Change Campaign Committee.
