Hong Kong poll sees record high turnout.

Voting for SAR's Election Committee hailed as indication of a maturing electoral system

The 2026 Election Committee Subsector Ordinary Elections on Sunday in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region returned 723 winners from 856 candidates across 31 subsectors for the new Election Committee, with a record 98.79 percent turnout.

Electoral Affairs Commission Chairman David Lok Kai-hong hailed the turnout as "encouraging", saying that it shows Hong Kong's electoral system is maturing.

The new term of the Election Committee will begin in February 2027. The committee is tasked with nominating the city's chief executive and Legislative Council members, as well as electing the chief executive and 40 of the council's 90 lawmakers. The election of the chief executive is scheduled for March 2027.

In a statement on Sunday night, Hong Kong's Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu congratulated these winners and thanked the voters for actively casting their votes and electing the best among the best as their preferred patriots in the Election Committee, thereby supporting Hong Kong's future development and reforms, including the implementation of Hong Kong's First Five-Year Plan (2026-30).

"It is my expectation that in accordance with the principle of 'patriots administering Hong Kong', all Election Committee members will fulfill their duties diligently and responsibly, and contribute to the steady and sustained implementation of the 'one country, two systems' principle," Lee said.

"The elections fully demonstrated a healthy competition among patriots who are committed, competent and constructive, highlighting the positivity of democratic development under the principle of 'patriots administering Hong Kong'," Lee said.

According to official data, 5,640 eligible voters cast ballots, with over 10 subsectors recording 100 percent turnout. Notably, by 1 pm — four hours after voting began — turnout had already surpassed the 89.77 percent recorded in the previous election in 2021.

Starting in the morning, officials inspected polling stations across the city and noted a lively atmosphere and good order.

Secretary for Constitutional and Mainland Affairs Janice Tse Siu-wa said that she was delighted to see the lively atmosphere at the polling stations, adding that many voters had lined up early, and that polling had been orderly and smooth.

Tse said that she expected the candidates to be "capable, visionary talents who can truly represent their sectors and the wider public interest". She added that she believes the election will help the Hong Kong SAR to better integrate into and serve the country's overall development and, ultimately, benefit the city's residents.

Secretary for Security Chris Tang Ping-keung said in a social media post that the elections had proceeded "smoothly and are in good order", after inspecting two polling stations in the morning.

He said that in order to ensure the smooth completion of polling, an emergency monitoring and support center had been activated to monitor conditions at polling stations.

Tang said the Election Committee plays a key role in Hong Kong's electoral system and is significant in promoting good governance.

Secretary for Home and Youth Affairs Alice Mak Mei-kuen, after inspecting the polling station at Harbour Road Sports Centre in Wan Chai, said the atmosphere of the election embodied the city's advanced democratic development.

Allan Zeman, chairman of Lan Kwai Fong Group, told China Daily that he cast his vote at the Wan Chai polling station, saying the process took him only 10 minutes with the use of automated machines. The businessman expressed his hope that the incoming Election Committee members will bear in mind the job prospects of Hong Kong's young people.

The elections on Sunday were the second ordinary elections of the committee since the electoral system was reformed in 2021.

Among the 1,500 members of the committee, 156 seats are nominated by designated bodies, and 982 are elected by eligible corporate or individual voters of the corresponding subsectors. A total of 362 are ex-officio members — including the city's members of the National People's Congress and the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — for which no election is required.

Among the 1,115 candidates, 259 from five subsectors were returned uncontested, and the remaining 856 from 31 contested subsectors competed for 723 seats.
