3. GOP uses 9/11 anniversary to bash left-wing Dems.

Photo illustration: Sarah Grillo/Axios. Photos: Henny Ray Abrams/AFP, Jeff Kowalsky/AFP, Finn Gomez via Getty Images

DALLAS — Republicans are invoking 9/11 to bash left-wing Democrats in a growing number of midterm races , seizing on the 25th anniversary of the attacks to portray them or their supporters as anti-American and sympathetic to terrorists.

Why it matters: It marks the return of a controversial political tactic that was common in the years after the 9/11 attacks but had largely faded from campaigns over the past quarter-century.

Driving the news: The strategy is playing out most prominently in Michigan, where Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers is expected to use his speech at the GOP's midterm convention to assail Democrat Abdul El-Sayed over 9/11, Axios has learned.

Rogers and his allies have saturated the Michigan race with 9/11-focused attacks in the days leading up to Friday's anniversary.

The other side: El-Sayed, a critic of Israel's government whose nomination was a victory for the rising left wing of the Democratic Party, has repeatedly rejected Piker's 2019 comment and said America didn't deserve to be attacked.

"Abdul has always unequivocally condemned terrorism and the September 11 attacks," El-Sayed spokesperson Roxie Richner told Axios.

While taking back his 2019 comments , Piker has continued to defend his underlying argument that U.S. foreign policy over several decades helped create the conditions that led to the attacks.

What they're saying: Matt Schlapp, who was George W. Bush's White House political director on 9/11, said the GOP strategy was smart — but needed to be handled carefully because the party seeks to court Arab voters.

Zoom out: Republicans are invoking 9/11 in campaigns beyond Michigan.

Between the lines: The GOP's 9/11-focused barrage is part of a broader effort to brand Democrats as extreme and increasingly influenced by the party's left wing.

Flashback: Republicans routinely cited 9/11 and terrorism in campaigning against Democrats immediately after the attacks.
