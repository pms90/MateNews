The math behind a Democratic wave — or a GOP upset.

Redistricting has helped House Republicans build a seawall that can withstand a roughly 5-point Democratic wave.

Why it matters: Early readings are pointing to something twice that size — enough to wash away the GOP House majority.

Democrats have been running roughly 10 points ahead of their 2024 presidential performance in special elections this year.

Between the lines: Gerrymandering since the presidential election has given Republicans more breathing room , with GOP-drawn maps creating a net advantage of roughly nine seats.

By the numbers: The current generic ballot has Democrats up roughly 6 points , which would produce a 221-214 Democratic majority in the Axios analysis.

By comparison: In 2018, the national House vote swung 6½ points toward Democrats from the 2016 presidential baseline. Democrats picked up 40 seats, producing a 235-199 majority (one seat was unresolved).

The other side: If Republicans outperform Trump's 2024 numbers by 5 points across congressional districts, the new maps would give them a massive 253-182 majority.

State of play: Two months before Election Day, Democrats can taste the House majority, provided two conditions hold: President Trump remains unpopular , and vulnerable Republicans stay close to him.

Go deeper: Individual House candidates can buck national trends and fare better than their party does — both nationally and in their individual districts.

The bottom line: Democrats are outperforming Republicans on nearly every metric but money.
