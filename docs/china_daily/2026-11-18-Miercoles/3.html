Event to showcase GBA development.

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, a gateway between China and the rest of the world, is well poised to become a platform for regional cooperation in advanced fields like trade digitalization and quantum security, turning consensus into practical standards and legal certainty for the Asia-Pacific economies, the city's alternate representatives to the Business Advisory Council of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation said on Tuesday.

They also said China's hosting APEC for the third time — also the first time that the APEC Economic Leaders' Week comes to the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area — offers the world a window to feel the 11-city cluster's development vibe.

The council, the sole standing advisory body under the APEC framework representing the commercial and industrial sectors, wrapped up its fourth and final meeting this year — part of the APEC Economic Leaders' Week — in Shenzhen on Monday.

Together with three earlier meetings in Jakarta, Mexico City and Bangkok, this year the council produced a record 14 documents to strengthen economic cooperation across the Asia-Pacific region.

Duncan Chiu, an alternate representative to the council, who is also a Hong Kong legislator representing the technology and innovation sector, said: "The APEC framework provides a platform for discussions on consensus-building for technology legislation and standards, helping slower economies keep pace."

In this respect, Hong Kong SAR can serve as China's interface with international markets for technology standards, exports and regulation, he added.

Chiu also emphasized quantum security preparedness, warning that divergent standards could expose communications and financial systems.

Another alternate representative of the SAR to the council, Geoffrey Kao, also pointed out the importance of addressing quantum-related risks.

Earlier this year, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority surveyed the SAR's banking sector to assess its preparedness for quantum-safe cryptography, with an initial assessment scoring the sector 2.3 out of 10.

"Many people aren't aware that quantum technology could be a potential cyber risk," Kao said.

The HKMA wants to drive the sector towards full quantum readiness — a full score of 10 by 2030. Kao noted that Hong Kong is "very ahead of our time in asking our banks to be ready", and "wants to extend this experience to all regions to help them prepare".

Kao also said the world is in a revolution in which economies shift from traditional models to technology-driven ones, and for regional hubs like Hong Kong, recognizing patent value lays the foundation for enjoying dividends of technology.

Kao said he hopes a region-wide patent evaluation standard can be launched in 2028. "Some economies have introduced their own measures, but they vary somewhat. APEC meetings would be very good platforms to bring the standard as one, where we can use this across borders," he said.

During the council's gathering in Shenzhen, members also systematically discussed the Greater Bay Area's economic landscape and potential for collaboration between different bay areas across the region.

Both Chiu and Kao believe hosting APEC meetings is a good chance for the 11-city cluster to showcase development strengths, greet friends, and demonstrate how the cities in the region complement each other.
