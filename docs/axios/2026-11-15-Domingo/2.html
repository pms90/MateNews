Space data centers could end stargazing as we know it.

Sprawling networks of space data centers could make it nearly impossible for humans to stargaze.

Why it matters: Most Americans don't want data centers built near them, but putting them in space brings a new set of problems.

Threat level: "Companies and policymakers should proceed with great caution," top academic researchers warn in a report for the Innovation for Cool Earth Forum, an annual event hosted by the Japanese government.

What they're saying: Columbia University's David Sandalow, the report's lead author, told Axios there are still "unknown unknowns" about space data center impacts.

By the numbers: Despite those concerns, investors and tech innovators are all in on space data centers.

Yes, but: Caleb Henry, Quilty Space's director of research, said that companies may be filing for more satellites than they will likely field. "They don't want to have to bump the number higher up later on," he said in an interview.

The bottom line: Researchers warned that governments and companies need to wait until space data center "risks and impacts are better understood and well managed" before putting them in orbit.
