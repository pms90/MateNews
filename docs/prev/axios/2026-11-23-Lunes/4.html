Scoop: The Pentagon's case for Canada to "get serious" on defense.

Canadian Defense Minister David McGuinty meets with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth at the Pentagon in September 2025. Photo: Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images

The Pentagon accused Canada of failing to become a "credible partner" due to its faltering military readiness and low defense spending, according to a sharply worded fact sheet issued by the Pentagon to justify new restrictions on America's northern neighbor.

Why it matters: The restrictions deepen tensions between the U.S. and Canada over trade and defense, as Ottawa reconsiders billions of dollars in purchases of American weapons.

Zoom in: At the heart of the dispute are NATO defense spending goals and military readiness under the bilateral North American Aerospace Defense Command where, the memo says, Canada falls short in three ways:

🛩️ Air: Only 41.9% of the Royal Canadian Air Force fleet "was serviceable from 2025-2026, largely due to ageing equipment and personnel shortages," the Pentagon fact sheet says. The shortfall limits Canada's "ability to meaningfully contribute to air defense commitments … to defend our North American airspace against all threats."

⚓️ Sea: Just 53.9% of Canada's naval fleet was "serviceable" from 2025-2026, limiting its "ability to monitor maritime approaches to North America," the fact sheet says.

🪖 Land: The Canadian Army had an operational readiness rate of 48% from 2025-2026.

The other side: Canada's own defense department reported the same equipment shortages while finding that 61% of its military units were ready for operations.

Friction point : NATO defense spending has been a constant sore spot for President Trump. Canada first agreed to spend 2% of GDP on defense spending in 2006, but it was only at 1.5% until 2025, according to the Pentagon.

The big picture: More defense spending by NATO allies is a revenue generator for the U.S., which has the globe's largest military-industrial complex.

Context: Relations between the two countries soured when Trump scrapped a free-trade agreement he insisted on in his first term, began a tariff war and threatened to annex Canada as the 51st state.

The bottom line: The two nations remain allies and work closely together on defense issues. Canada has an office in the Pentagon next to the joint staff.
