"Stop gaslighting me!": Florida Democrats' Senate nominee and party chair have heated call over socialism.

Angie Nixon campaigning this week. Photo: Joe Raedle/Getty Images

Florida's new Democratic Senate nominee, Angie Nixon , and the state party's chair had a heated conversation Friday that included a yelling match about the impact of socialism and the challenges of campaigning in a red state like Florida, three people familiar with the conversation tell Axios.

Why it matters: The conflict exposes the fault line in the party between the progressive base embodied by Nixon and the establishment represented by the state party's chair, Nikki Fried.

Zoom in: Nixon, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America who won the primary in a shocking upset over Alex Vindman earlier this week, expressed frustrations to Fried that she had not received sufficient help from the state party.

According to two people who spoke with Nixon, both she and Fried yelled at each other during the call. Another person who spoke to Fried said the chair did not yell but was yelled at.

The other side: A Florida Democratic Party official disputed that Fried yelled at Nixon.

Between the lines: A second person who talked to Nixon described the feud as "a clash of ideology — but it's also a clash of personalities."

Another Florida Democratic official with knowledge of the dispute said Nixon and Fried have never been friendly and that Fried "was very disappointed that Angie won. She made that pretty well known."

The big picture: Fried's concerns about socialism and its potential effect in the general election are widely shared in the Democratic Party, from Florida to Washington.

The intrigue: What caught Nixon off guard, she told others, was that she was in the process of distinguishing herself from the DSA anyway.

So far, Nixon has had an easier time finding common ground with David Jolly, the ex-Republican now running as the Democratic nominee for governor.

In addition to facing Republican Byron Donalds who seeks to be the first Black governor in Florida history with $100 million already raised, Jolly is contesting with an independent run by Desmond Meade, the Black man who successfully restored voting rights to felons.
