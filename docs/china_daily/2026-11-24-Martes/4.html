Joint welfare plan to cover young and old.

New model aims to offer affordable, quality eldercare and nursery services

China will develop a "joint elderly-child care" model, which allows community facilities to care for elderly people and young children under one roof, according to a plan unveiled on Monday.

The plan, issued by the National Development and Reform Commission and three other central government departments, aims to raise the coverage rate of community eldercare institutions and facilities to 70 percent by 2030.

By then, the proportion of nursing beds in eldercare institutions is expected to reach 73 percent, while the enrollment rate of children under 3 in nursery services will increase by 6 percentage points from 2025 levels.

The plan said the layout of eldercare and nursery facilities will be further optimized, with an inclusive eldercare and nursery service system — covering urban and rural areas and offering affordable prices and guaranteed quality — basically taking shape.

To better meet demand for eldercare facilities, China will focus on upgrading 2,000 public eldercare institutions that have aging and outdated facilities, while advancing the construction of regional eldercare service centers in townships and subdistricts. Eligible localities will also be supported in building eldercare institutions adjacent to medical and health institutions, according to the plan.

To ease the burden of raising children, China will advance the construction of comprehensive nursery service centers at the city level and in major populous counties. It will also support qualified kindergartens in offering classes for children aged 2 to 3 and create a "15-minute nursery service circle", the plan said.

Nursery institutions will be encouraged to extend their services to rural areas where there is demand, while employers will be encouraged to cooperate with professional institutions to provide nursery services using their own facilities, it said.

The plan also encourages embedded community service facilities to serve multiple purposes and provide half-day and hourly care.

As part of the renovation of older residential communities, priority will be given to installing or upgrading elevators in buildings with many elderly residents. Public places and transportation facilities will also undergo age-friendly, child-friendly and barrier-free upgrades, the plan said.

By the end of 2025, China had 395,000 eldercare institutions and facilities with 7.68 million beds, as well as 126,000 nursery institutions, with the number of nursery slots totaling 4.73 per 1,000 people, according to official data.

Han Ruidong, an associate researcher at the State Information Center of the National Development and Reform Commission, said China's population development is now characterized by an aging population, declining births and differentiated regional population growth, making it more urgent to build a population service system covering all people and the entire life cycle.

Eldercare and childcare concern the well-being of hundreds of millions of families and the country's long-term development, Han said, calling for coordinated efforts across regions and departments, better use of funding channels and an improved regulatory system to support the development and sustainable operation of the service system.

For Liu, a 40-year-old man in Changsha, Hunan province, the plan comes as welcome relief. With two children aged 5 and 1 and parents approaching 70, he said the "joint elderly-child care model" and the 15-minute nursery service circle would make daily life more manageable for families like his.

"Being able to drop off my youngest at a nearby nursery and have my parents cared for in the same community would save us so much time and stress," he said.

He added that the emphasis on upgrading older residential communities, including installing elevators in buildings with many elderly residents, is especially meaningful for his parents, who live on a higher floor and find climbing stairs increasingly difficult.
