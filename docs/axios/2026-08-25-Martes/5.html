5. Trump's Cabinet goes full creator.

RFK Jr. speaks with Andrew Gruel, a chef and local California politician. Screenshot: The Real Food Show on YouTube

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy have both launched YouTube shows in recent weeks, the latest examples of how the Trump administration is redefining how government communicates with the American public.

Why it matters: The White House has bypassed traditional press relations while assuming an unorthodox approach to media that puts itself in the role of content creator.

Zoom in: In " The Real Food Show ," Kennedy plays celebrity chef, donning an apron to make healthy meals alongside guests.

Between the lines: Other sitting officials have started experimenting with podcasts, which has become more common among elected politicians.

Reality check: Although President Trump — the original reality TV star-turned-politician — successfully built his career off of "The Apprentice" and other media ventures, his Cabinet officials have seen mixed results as content creators.

Zoom out: While previous administrations officials have done one-off influencer collaborations or Reddit AMA conversations , few have ventured into the territory of highly produced video series starring themselves.

The big picture: For Trump administration officials, personal vlogs and podcasts can serve as an opportunity to use a more casual tone that resembles the president's communication style, with the hope that it will resonate with everyday Americans.

The bottom line: By putting themselves at the center of their own creative messaging, administration officials are able to sidestep traditional media gatekeepers while appealing to their bosses' messaging style.
