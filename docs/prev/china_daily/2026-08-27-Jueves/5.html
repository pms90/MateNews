Chongqing brings fresh produce to rail commuters.

Chongqing is redefining urban living with the recent launch of a farmers market right next to the city subway's Congyansi Station exit on Line 9. This initiative has been warmly received by residents, transforming their daily commutes into vibrant, community-focused experiences.

Situated in Chongqing's Liangjiang New Area, the area around the station features both large residential complexes and expansive rural areas. The farmers market provides local farmers with a dedicated space to sell seasonal produce, including vegetables, fruits, free-range eggs, grains and a variety of handmade products.

This development addresses a long-standing challenge farmers have faced in finding permanent venues for trade, while offering residents access to fresh, affordable produce without the need to travel long distances to large supermarkets.

The Chongqing Rail Transit Operation Co, part of Chongqing City Transportation Development and Investment Group, identified this gap, repurposed idle space around the station and transformed it into a bustling marketplace.

Vendors like Zhan Zuxiang, a 63-year-old who previously sold tofu outdoors, appreciate the market's comfort and convenience. "It is much cooler here than outside," she said.

Similarly, Zhang Xuyao, a 28-year-old bakery stall owner, said that promotional efforts have attracted a growing number of customers. "After I moved in, the market operator also offered some promotional activities and other incentives to attract customers. More people are coming to the farmers market."

Nearby residents have welcomed the market for its convenience and nostalgic appeal. A shopper, surnamed Huang, likened it to the traditional markets of his childhood, saying, "It's close to home and brings back fond memories."

The market is not just a collection of stalls; it is a fully standardized and regulated operation. Wu Tao, deputy general manager of Chongqing Rail Transit Operation Co, emphasized the market's focus on safety and orderliness, ensuring that key transit spaces remain unobstructed, and all operations comply with safety standards.

The market partner leverages its extensive operational experience to provide comprehensive services, ensuring product quality and managing customer flow, laying a foundation for long-term success.

The farmers market is just one example of Chongqing's innovative use of idle space managed by the city's rail operator. At Yudong Station on CRT Line 3 in the city's Banan district, unused areas have been transformed into a community sports facility, featuring a badminton court. Wu added that they have also converted other large, idle spaces around the transit lines into a clothing market and a senior care hospital. These initiatives showcase Chongqing's commitment to enhancing urban life by turning idle spaces into vibrant community hubs.

The city continues to win hearts with its warmth and vibrant local culture. In early 2024, it allowed farmers to carry large bamboo baskets filled with vegetables on Line 4, affectionately known as the "Vegetable Basket Line", during peak hours. Despite some initial complaints about space issues, this policy enables farmers to sell their produce at higher prices in the city, further integrating rural and urban life.
