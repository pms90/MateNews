Trump's AI bot gives mixed answers on 2020 election, Jan. 6.

Photo illustration: Brendan Lynch/Axios. Photo: Jakub Porzycki/NurPhoto via Getty Images

The Trump administration's new AI service gave users conflicting responses to politically sensitive questions Tuesday and Wednesday: sometimes answering them and sometimes refusing.

The big picture: As the Trump administration increasingly uses AI and answers the public's questions through America.gov, statements carrying the government's authority are shaped by models, instructions and guardrails the public can't see.

Driving the news: On Tuesday, Axios — like other outlets — found America.gov contradicted the president on the 2020 election and the Jan. 6 attack.

Yes, but: By Wednesday morning, some users were getting a different response: The chatbot refused to answer, saying it did not provide "political commentary."

What they're saying: There are several plausible explanations for the inconsistent responses, tech experts explain, but without specifics about how America.gov is configured, it's impossible to know which, if any, were responsible.

But user prompts also have an impact: Sometimes, different framing received different responses. For example, asked this morning in Jacksonville, Florida, on a Chrome incognito window:

State of play: And now, at the time of this article's publication, I'm getting responses similar to those shortly after launch: Biden won in 2020, Obama was born in Hawaii and people broke into the Capitol on Jan. 6, 2021.

There could be benign reasons for the fluctuation, explains David Nesting, the former deputy chief information officer at the Office of Personnel Management.

Yes, but: The GSA did not immediately respond to Axios' request for information about America.gov's guardrails.

Friction point: It could result from a game of probability, says Rumman Chowdhury, the founding director of the Independent AI Evaluation Foundation. But the guardrails could "also be shaping the output to be ideologically aligned with what the president or his party wants."

The stakes will soon get higher: The next iteration of America.gov, set for early 2027, will integrate across agencies so users can complete forms, applications and renewals — like enrolling in Medicare, applying for a passport or applying for federal jobs.
