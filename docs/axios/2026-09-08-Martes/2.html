2. Axios C-Suite: The 4 ways that AI could play out.

Every CEO needs to be super-fluent in AI advancements and planning for wild scenarios — ranging from true superintelligence in 2027 to a political movement to shut it down.

Why it matters: This isn't something we can outsource, slow-walk or ignore. The technology and politics are shifting so fast we need to scramble to understand — and ideally routinely use — it.

Consider this brief your frame for thinking through the four most plausible emerging scenarios:

1. The techno-optimists are right. So far, AI capabilities have advanced faster than almost anyone predicted. Yes, the tech is imperfect — but it would be foolish not to assume it keeps getting meaningfully better with each passing month. This is my dominant assumption.

2. An AI catastrophe hits. I work under the assumption that something big and bad will hit in the next 18 months, most likely a widespread cyber attack or bio-engineered virus set loose by a bad actor.

3. Shut-it-down politics explode . If something bad happens — or even if data centers keep rising in unpopularity, or job losses start to mount — Democrats seem ready to turn hard against AI. Bernie Sanders, the dominant voice of the left, has already called for a pause. He introduced legislation that would imprison anyone building superintelligence. In doing so, Sanders was joined by Rep. Greg Casar (D-Texas), the head of the Congressional Progressive Caucus, which includes 95 of 214 House Dems.

4. The messy middle persists. This is a very plausible — and some think most likely — outcome. AI keeps advancing, but not so fast or transformationally as the optimists think. Our employees use it more and more, but it simply takes longer to connect to existing data, systems and security layers to truly upend work and life as we know it.
