3. Lula v. Bolsonaro: Brazil election unfolds in Trump's shadow.

Combination images of Brazil's President and re-election candidate Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and President Trump. Photo by Aaron Schwartz and Sergio LIMA / AFP via Getty Images

Brazil votes Sunday in a presidential election that could reshape its relationship with President Trump , return the Bolsonaro movement to power and alter the future of the Amazon.

Why it matters: The showdown between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro has turned into something bigger: a test of Trump's influence in Latin America's largest country, with stakes for the region and the global climate.

The latest: Trump said Thursday he's following Brazil's election "very closely" and called it "very important," though he didn't endorse a candidate.

Catch up quick: Trump inserted the U.S. into the contest when his administration imposed additional 25% tariffs on some Brazilian goods this summer — prompting Lula to accuse the American leader of "interference" in Brazil's affairs.

Context: The confrontation escalated in 2025 when the Trump administration targeted Brazilian Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes , who oversaw the prosecution of the elder Bolsonaro.

State of play: Jair Bolsonaro is barred from running, but his son has pledged to continue his father's political legacy.

The big picture: Nearly 60% of the Amazon rainforest is in Brazil. The forest stores enormous amounts of carbon and helps regulate the climate, giving the next president influence over an ecosystem with consequences far beyond Brazil's borders.

Lula's pitch: The 80-year-old president is campaigning on his economic and social policy record : state-led national development, income redistribution and continuing welfare programs.

Bolsonaro's pitch: The 45-year-old lawyer, who has represented Rio de Janeiro in Brazil's Senate since 2019, is campaigning on conservative social policies, tax cuts and a tougher approach to crime.

What's next: Brazil votes Sunday. If no candidate wins more than 50% of valid votes, the top two finishers will advance to an Oct. 25 runoff.
