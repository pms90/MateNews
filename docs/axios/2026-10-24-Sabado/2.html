Washington's message to AI execs: please shut up.

Washington has always viewed Silicon Valley as culturally strange, but the current crop of AI leaders is becoming a political liability, sources close to the White House and Congress tell Axios.

Why it matters : Lawmakers, hill staffers and Washington insiders in both parties wish AI founders would do what every other major industry chief does: put a sock in it and just build their products — safely.

State of play : Rather than leading the charge to persuade Americans about the benefits of AI, and the potential for shared prosperity — the core elements of any winning political campaign — industry leaders are constantly warning of its existential dangers.

What they're saying: Lawmakers say the message from AI execs too often alternates between prosperity and doom, sometimes in the same public appearances. The whiplash creates adds to political instability in the face of an already powerful public backlash, lawmakers and regulators say.

Democrats also have paid close attention to just how much top AI and tech leaders have cozied up to President Trump. Many see opportunity in turning AI gaffes into an easy political win.

The big picture: Friday night news dumps and consequential weekend blog posts have become Anthropic and OpenAI's bread and butter.

Behind the scenes: Administration officials are once again disgruntled with Anthropic, for two reasons.

Anthropic says it briefed the White House and affected agencies on the safety incidents once their reviews were complete, and it is working with the Super Intelligence Force as it develops an incident reporting process.

Zoom in: Washington's headaches with AI execs also extend to OpenAI, and both companies are targets of a developing FTC investigation.

Reality check : Those efforts have so far failed to win hearts and minds, with another recent poll showing a majority of Americans see AI as bad for society.

The bottom line: For AI CEOs, the lesson is increasingly clear: do less.
