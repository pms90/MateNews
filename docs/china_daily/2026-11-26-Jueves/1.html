Beijing-Irkutsk train service gets on track.

Rail link designed for extreme cold and long-distance cross-border travel

An international passenger train carrying nearly 200 people left Beijing on Wednesday for Irkutsk, a Russian city near Lake Baikal, via Mongolia, launching a weekly rail service linking China, Mongolia and Russia.

More than 100 passengers aboard the inaugural V3 service were international travelers. The train left Beijing Railway Station at 7:27 am and is scheduled to arrive in Irkutsk on Friday afternoon after a 2,666-kilometer journey lasting about 55 hours.

The service passes through Ulaanbaatar, the capital of Mongolia. One highlight of the journey comes after the train enters Russia, where it travels along the shore of Lake Baikal for about five hours, according to Liu Haijun, the V3 train's conductor.

The return train is scheduled to depart Irkutsk on Saturdays and arrive in Beijing on Mondays, according to China State Railway Group, the national railway operator.

The previous international passenger service linking China, Mongolia and Russia ran between Beijing and Moscow via Ulaanbaatar. Known as the K3/4 service, it was suspended in 2020. The relaunched service has a different train number and a shorter route, with Irkutsk as its terminus.

The train has 16 cars, including one dining car, 11 hard sleeper cars and two deluxe soft sleeper cars.

Zhu Naxin, an official at the Beijing rolling stock depot, said the train completed a cross-border test run in March 2025, following 20 months of preparation.

"The train is suited to extreme cold and long-distance travel, and meets the conditions for cross-border operation," Zhu said.

On the former international service, passengers were mainly served by Mongolian and Russian dining cars after the train left China, Liu Haijun said. On the new service, the Chinese dining car crosses the border with the train and remains on board throughout the round trip.

"Chinese passengers who are not accustomed to Western food can choose Chinese meals throughout the journey, while international passengers can also enjoy authentic Chinese dishes on board," he said.

Among the passengers on the inaugural run was Liu Huan, a deputy general manager of a Zhejiang-based travel agency, who was traveling with her child. Her company had sent her to explore the route and had already prepared a tour package, with plans to take visitors to Russia in April or May next year.

"With visa-free policies, inbound and outbound tourism has become very popular," she said. "We hope to bring more and more tourists to Russia by international train to experience the country."

Zhou Xiangfeng, an onboard interpreter with the international train crew, helps passengers overcome language barriers, navigate border procedures and answer questions during the journey.

"Passengers who encounter difficulties on the train can ask the attendants in their cars to find me," Zhou said.

At Beijing Railway Station, a dedicated entrance and waiting area were set up for international passengers. Signs in Chinese and Russian provided information about rules governing items carried across the border.

One-way individual fares from Beijing to Irkutsk are 2,411 yuan ($361) for a hard sleeper and 3,763 yuan for a deluxe soft sleeper, according to ticket sales information. Discounted fares are available for children and groups of at least six traveling together.

Tickets for the international journey are not available through the regular 12306 booking platform.
